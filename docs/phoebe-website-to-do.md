## 2. A real lesson photo

**Why:** This is the biggest thing missing from the whole site. There isn't a single photo of a lesson actually happening. One good photo of you teaching a student would do more for the site than any design change.

**Ideas for the shot:**
- You sitting beside a student at the piano, both looking at the music
- A close-up of small hands on the keys with your hand nearby guiding
- A student playing while you listen, smiling

**Permission:** you **must** get written permission from the parent before using any photo of a child. A simple signed note or email saying "I give permission for Kreutzer to use photos of [child] on its website" is enough. Keep a copy.

**If you'd rather not show a child's face,** shots from behind or over the shoulder, or close-ups of hands, work really well and need less permission worry.

**Take both:** a **landscape (horizontal)** version and a **portrait (vertical)** version of your favourite setup, so the site has options.

## 3. Studio reshoot

**Why:** The current studio photo is used in four places. It's bright and flat, with white walls and a brown cover draped over the piano. The other photos on the site are dark, moody and dramatic, so the studio photo stands out as looking less polished. It's the only real photo of the space, so it should look its best.

**How to get a great shot:**
- **Remove the piano cover** and **open the piano lid** (full stick if possible)
- Shoot in the **late afternoon or evening** with **warm lamps on** and the overhead lights off or dimmed
- Put some sheet music on the stand, maybe a pencil and a vase or plant nearby
- Tidy cables, bags and anything unrelated to music out of frame
- Take it from a few angles: one wide shot of the whole room, one closer shot of the piano and acoustic panels
- **No flash.** Hold the phone very still or rest it on something
- Take **landscape (horizontal)** shots. These are used as wide banners.
